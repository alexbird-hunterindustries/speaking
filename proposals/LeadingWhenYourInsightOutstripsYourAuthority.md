# "I Can't Practice <Good Idea> Because My Team Doesn't Want To"

Strategies for shaping your team's work towards iterative, test-driven, customer-focused processes (when you're not the
boss.)

There is a special kind of frustration that comes from having experience or insight that would benefit your work, but
not having the authority to employ it. We learn about all kinds of collaborative work practices -- involving our peers,
stakeholders, and customers to radically improve the outcome of our work -- but how can we introduce those practices
when nobody wants to listen?

In this talk, I'll share practices and anecdotes to earn trust and share practices when you can't command people to work
with you. Today, I find these techniques especially valuable when I'm working with folks who don't know me, when I'm
working in an environment with a strong tradition of doing things the way we do things, or when I have formal authority
but want to invite my team instead of commanding them.

We'll cover introducing modeling techniques like User Story Mapping, Event Storming, and DDD Context Mapping. We'll also
cover introducing coding patterns -- TDD, hex architecture, etc. -- when nobody else is interested. Finally, we'll talk
about manouevers to get customer feedback when you don't have access to customers.

These things are simpler if you can tell everyone what to do, but the techniques for using influence instead of
authority is useful whether or not you have the power to command.
